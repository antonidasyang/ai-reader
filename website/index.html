J. RES. READING · VOL. 12147
Attention and the Craft of Reading Research
L. Chen  ·  M. Okada  ·  School of Information Science

1  INTRODUCTION

Researchers spend a large share of their working hours with the literature, yet the craft of reading itself is rarely taught. A paper is not a linear text: its argument lives in the abstract, its truth in the tables, and its caveats in the margins.

A structured reading proceeds in passes: a first pass for the shape of the argument, a second for its evidence, and a third for the details that resist summary. Most time is lost not in reading, but in re-reading without a map.

2  METHOD

We observed sixty graduate students across three cohorts as they worked through an unfamiliar corpus, recording where attention settled and where it slipped. Sections were annotated against a taxonomy of reading intents.

The resulting traces suggest that comprehension follows structure: readers who recovered the outline of a paper first retained more of its argument, at every level of expertise we measured.
